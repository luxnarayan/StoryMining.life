Your deck has your credentials.

The room remembers the stories.

Your people have done far more than they can remember. Clients only hear what they can recall in the room.

Twenty years of engagements sit in credential slides, and clients skim them. What they remember is the one story told over coffee, the one that made the person across the table sound like someone who has seen this before.

That person picks from whatever comes to mind on the day. For most people it is the same five stories, on rotation, for years. The rest is buried under the weeds of the work.

As AI makes products and proposals look alike, the client is choosing the person across the table.

A client can't fully inspect what they're buying until after they've bought it, so they read the person. What they are reading is judgment, and judgment shows up as stories. I've seen this before. Here's what happened. Here's what we did.

A person's stories are the one thing a competitor can't copy.

Same tools. Same decks. Same pitch.

Different stories.

Your competitors have the same AI. Only you have your people's experience.

Available to everyone

Proposals, decks and follow-ups can be drafted in minutes by anyone. As that gets cheaper, polish stops separating one company from another, and the choice moves to something else.

Only yours

What's left is what your people have lived: the engagements, the calls they got right, the things they watched go wrong. Almost none of it is written down anywhere, so no model has it. Once the stories are out, AI can help shape them, and the raw material stays yours.

What happens in the workshop

Name, Place, Animal, Thing

Map

Places lived and worked down the side, the names you answered to across the top: employee, manager, founder, parent. Most of the grid stays empty. What's left is your territory.

Mine

Two probes bring each era back: what you did with your body and breath, and what you made with your hands. Four lenses turn one memory into several stories.

Cut

Name the feeling, find the shape and the turn, and keep the stories worth telling.

Show

The same story runs differently in a first call, a boardroom and over dinner. What moves is where the turn sits.

Work stories and personal stories

Client conversations need both
Work

Stories that carry judgment

The engagement that went sideways, the call you got right, the client who changed your mind.

Personal

Stories that carry who you are

They let the client meet the person before they hire the firm.

Both

The best ones are both

Think of the client call you took on the way to a family wedding. It is a work story, told by a person, and the client hears both.

Everyone chooses which personal stories to share in the room. The rest stay theirs.

The thread
My grandfather had people talking to a room. StreamAlive has a room talking to people.

That line surfaced from my own scattered stories: a grandfather who taught public speaking, improv, stand-up, reading a live chat. I hadn't connected them until I saw them side by side.

Once the stories are out, each person can see what they stand for. Across a team, those threads add up to a firm voice that competitors can't copy.
