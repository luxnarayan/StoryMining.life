AI can write your post, your deck, your bio. It cannot write your life.

Ten, twenty, thirty years in, you're carrying more lived experience than you can account for. Rooms you sat in. Decisions that went wrong in interesting ways. The thing you do on weekends that explains you better than your job title.

That pile can't be generated, copied or outsourced. Everyone's output is converging — same tools, same tone, same credentials in the same room. Lived experience is what's left, and almost none of it has been examined.

Mine — the two probes

Animal & thing

The grid tells you where and who. The probes tell you what it felt like to be there. Animal — what you did with your body, your brain, your breath. Thing — what you did with your hands. Neither is the story. Both bring the memory back in colour.

Dubai · Father
AnimalOff-roading, most weekends
ThingA car permanently full of sand

Kids eating sand in the desert. With the first one, two young parents obsessing. With the second, "nah, it's fine." Same desert, four years apart.

Same city, same year, two different names — two stories with nothing in common. That's the grid earning its keep.

Mine — the four lenses

The multiplier

Each seed gets asked four questions. One coordinate can yield four unrelated stories.

Point

It happened to me.

Line

It happened to someone right beside me.

Square

It happened around me, in my vicinity.

Cube

It happened in the world, and I was somewhere when it did.

A Cube story is still told from the Point. The world event isn't the story — a room no one could reach, and a client who wanted to keep going, is. You're always the point of contact.
